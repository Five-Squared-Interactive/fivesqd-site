# About Five Squared Interactive

An independent studio building the pieces that let a 3D world be a web
page: a set of files at an address, opened in a browser, linked to other
worlds, and owned by whoever made it.

## Who we are (photo left, text right)

*(The founder's photo from his AWE USA 2026 speaker page,

**Dylan Baker**, founder, is the architect of Web Wide Worlds, a
framework for modular 3D spaces and accessible world-building on the open
web. His work includes WorldHub and WebVerse, and bridging games like
Minecraft and Cities: Skylines 2 into Web Wide Worlds, enabling new
interactions with existing creations. His background spans computer
networking, embedded systems and 3D visualization, with experience
leading spacecraft flight software development at NASA's Goddard Space
Flight Center and serving as lead architect of an XR platform for
aerospace engineering and scientific visualization. *(His AWE USA 2026

Five Squared Interactive is one person and the people who build with him.
Small, open-source first, building in public since 2019.

## What we believe

A world should outlive the company that helped make it. So the format is
open, the page that opens worlds is open, hosting is a convenience the
design never requires, and the design puts nothing of ours between a link
and the world it points to. A thousand worlds on our servers would mean
less to us than one person's world at their own address.

## How we got here

We have been building open 3D worlds for the web since 2019. The
long form of the idea is the 2025 white paper, Web Wide Worlds. Today a
Blender scene becomes a world in one press. The code is open source under
MIT, on GitHub.

- **In a paragraph.** A world made with Five Squared's tools is a small
  text file and the 3D models it names, served from an ordinary web
  address. Anyone with the link walks in from a browser and meets whoever
  else is there; a door in one world can open onto another, the way pages
  link. Today a Blender user publishes a scene as a world in one press,
  hosted on the company's WorldHub service or on any web host. The format
  (VEML) and the page that opens worlds (WebVerse) are open source under
